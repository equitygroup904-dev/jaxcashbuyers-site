Maybe it was your parents' place on a few acres off a quiet road. Maybe you bought it years ago as a starter home, and now you've outgrown it. Or maybe the roof, the floors and the skirting all need work at the same time, and you just don't have it in you anymore. Quick note before we start: we're not attorneys or tax advisors. This is general information only. Title questions, estates and taxes can get complicated, so talk with a real estate attorney or tax professional about your own situation.

Why Selling a Mobile Home Can Feel Harder Than Selling a House

Owners of mobile and manufactured homes often find out the hard way that the usual selling process doesn't fit them very well. A few reasons:

  • Financing is tougher for buyers. Many traditional buyers need a loan, and lenders tend to have stricter rules for manufactured homes, especially older ones. Fewer qualified buyers can mean a longer wait.
  • Condition matters a lot. Soft floors, roof leaks, plumbing under the home and worn-out skirting are common in older homes, and buyers notice.
  • The paperwork is different. A mobile home may have its own title, separate from the land. That surprises a lot of people.
  • Not every agent wants the listing. Some agents focus on site-built homes and may not be eager to market a mobile home on acreage.

None of this means you can't sell. It just means it helps to know what you're working with before you start.

Selling a Mobile Home With Land in Middleburg and Clay County

Middleburg and the rest of western Clay County have plenty of mobile and manufactured homes sitting on their own land, from small lots to several acres. If you own both the home and the land under it, you're in a different spot than someone who rents a lot in a park.

Here's the big question: is the home titled separately, or has the title been retired?

In Florida, a mobile home usually starts out with a title, a lot like a car. When the same person owns the home and the land, and the home is permanently attached, the owner can apply to "retire" that title so the home is treated as part of the real property. Your county tax collector's office handles this kind of title work.

Why does this matter when you sell?

  • If the title was retired, the home and land are generally sold together with a deed, like a regular house.
  • If the home still has its own title, the buyer usually needs both the deed to the land and the signed-over mobile home title.
  • If you can't find the title, or it's still in a late relative's name, that needs to be sorted out before or during the sale. A title company or attorney can help, and the Clay County Tax Collector's office can tell you what's needed to get a replacement.

Not sure which situation you're in? Look at your paperwork, check your property tax bill, or call the tax collector's office. A good title company will figure it out as part of any sale, too.

Selling a Mobile Home in a Park

If your home sits on a rented lot in a mobile home park, the land isn't part of the sale. You'd be selling just the home. A few things to keep in mind:

  • Florida's Mobile Home Act (Chapter 723 of the Florida Statutes) and your park's rules may give the park a say in approving a buyer who wants to keep the home on the lot. Read your lease and prospectus, and ask park management how their process works.
  • Back lot rent or fees owed to the park can complicate a sale. Know where you stand before you list.
  • Moving an older home out of a park is expensive and not always possible, so most park homes are sold to someone who will stay on that lot.

What to Gather Before You Sell

You don't need everything on this list to get started, but having it ready makes things move faster:

  1. The deed to the land (or the parcel number from your property tax bill).
  2. The mobile home title, if it hasn't been retired.
  3. Any mortgage or loan statements, so you know what's owed on the home or land.
  4. Recent property tax bills, and whether any taxes are behind.
  5. Well, septic and power details, if you know them.
  6. Probate or estate paperwork, if you inherited the property. Our guide to selling a house during probate covers the basics.
  7. A rough list of repairs you already know about.

Your Options for Selling

List it with an agent

If the home is in good shape and the title and land are clear, listing can work. You'll likely need to fix things up, allow showings and wait for a buyer whose financing gets approved. You'll also pay commissions and closing costs. For the right property, that can be worth it.

Sell it yourself

Some owners put up a sign or post online. That can save commissions, but you'll handle the calls, showings, paperwork and title questions on your own. It can also bring a lot of lowball offers and people who can't actually close.

A Few Honest Tips for Middleburg Mobile Home Owners

  • Don't sink money into big repairs before you talk to buyers. If you're selling as-is, a new roof or floors may not come back to you dollar for dollar.
  • Don't wait on title problems. If the title is lost, in a relative's name or never retired the way you thought, start sorting it out early. It's usually the thing that slows a sale down most.
  • Get more than one opinion. Talk to an agent and a cash buyer, then decide which path fits your timeline and goals.
