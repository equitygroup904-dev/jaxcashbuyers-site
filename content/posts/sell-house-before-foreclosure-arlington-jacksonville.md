## How Foreclosure Works for an Arlington, Jacksonville Home

Arlington is part of Jacksonville and Duval County, so a foreclosure on a house there runs through the Duval County court system.

Florida uses what's called **judicial foreclosure**. That means the lender can't just take the house. They have to file a lawsuit in court, and a judge has to sign off before the home can be sold at a foreclosure sale. Here is the general order of events most homeowners see:

1. **Missed payments.** After a payment is missed, the lender usually sends late notices and calls.
2. **Notice of default or intent to foreclose.** The lender sends a letter saying the loan is in default and what it takes to fix it.
3. **The lawsuit.** If the default isn't resolved, the lender files a foreclosure complaint and you are served with a summons.
4. **The judgment.** If the case goes against you, the judge enters a final judgment and sets a sale date.
5. **The foreclosure sale.** The property is auctioned through the Duval County Clerk of Courts.

How long each step takes depends on your lender, your loan, and the court's schedule. There is no set number of days that applies to everyone, so don't count on having more time than you do.

### Don't Ignore the Summons

If you are served with foreclosure papers, the summons gives you a deadline to respond. Missing that deadline can let the case move faster against you. Read every page and contact an attorney right away, even if you plan to sell.

## Can You Sell a House That's in Foreclosure?

In most cases, yes. Until the foreclosure sale happens, you usually still own the home and can sell it. The sale has to pay off what you owe the lender (plus any other liens), and the lender has to be paid at closing for the title company to clear the title.

Selling before the auction can help in a few ways:

- **You stay in control** of the price, the buyer, and the move-out date instead of the court deciding.
- **You may walk away with any equity** left after the loan and costs are paid. At an auction, that money can be harder to reach.
- **You avoid a completed foreclosure** on your record, which many homeowners want to keep off their credit history.

The earlier you start, the more choices you have. Once a sale date is close, there is less time for a buyer, a title search, and a closing to come together.

## Your Options When You're Behind on Payments

Selling isn't the only path. Depending on your loan and your income, some homeowners in Arlington look at these options first:

### Loan Modification or Repayment Plan

Some lenders will change the loan terms or let you catch up over time. This can work well if your income has recovered and you want to keep the house. Call your loan servicer and ask what programs are available.

### Reinstatement

Reinstating the loan means paying everything that's past due, plus fees, in one lump sum. If you can get the money together, this stops the foreclosure and keeps the original loan in place.

### Short Sale

If you owe more than the house is worth, a short sale means the lender agrees to accept less than the full balance. It requires the lender's approval and often takes time, so it's not always a fit when an auction date is close.

### Selling to a Cash Buyer

A cash buyer purchases the house as-is, with no repairs, no showings, and no waiting on a buyer's mortgage approval. A cash offer is usually less than full retail value, so it makes the most sense when time, condition, or simplicity matter more than getting top dollar.
