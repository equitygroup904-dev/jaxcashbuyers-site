## Why People Decide to Sell Vacant Land

Land is a little different from a house. It doesn't usually come with a tenant calling about a broken AC, but it still costs you money and attention. Here are some of the most common reasons owners decide to sell:

- **The property taxes keep coming.** Vacant land generally doesn't qualify for a homestead exemption, since nobody lives on it. The bill shows up every year whether you use the land or not.
- **You inherited it.** Many lots and acreage tracts in Nassau County pass down through families. If you live out of state, or several relatives share ownership, it can be hard to know what to do with it.
- **Your plans changed.** You were going to build a home or retire there, and now that's not happening.
- **You'd rather have the cash.** Money tied up in land you don't use could go toward a home, debt, retirement, or family.

None of these are bad reasons.

## Selling Land in Yulee, Callahan, Hilliard and the Rest of Nassau County

Nassau County is a mix of very different places. Yulee and the areas near Fernandina Beach have seen a lot of new homes and new roads over the years. Out west, around Callahan and Hilliard, you'll find more rural acreage, timberland, and wooded lots on quieter roads.

That variety matters when you sell. Two lots of the same size can be worth very different amounts depending on things like:

- Road frontage and legal access
- Whether utilities (power, water, sewer) are nearby or you'd need a well and septic
- Zoning and what the county will allow on the property
- Wetlands or flood zones on part or all of the parcel
- Whether the land is cleared, wooded, or has old structures on it

We can't tell you what your land is buildable for, and no honest buyer should promise that without checking. The Nassau County Planning and Zoning staff can tell you how your parcel is zoned. FEMA flood maps can show whether it's in a flood zone. A survey can confirm the boundaries and access.

## What to Gather Before You Sell Your Land

You don't need all of this to start, but it helps.

### 1. The parcel ID and legal description

You can usually find your parcel number on your tax bill or by searching your name or address on the Nassau County Property Appraiser's website. This lets any buyer look up the exact land you're talking about.

### 2. Proof you own it (and who else does)

Check who is listed on the deed. If a parent or grandparent is still listed and they've passed away, the land may need to go through probate or another legal step before it can be sold. If that's your situation, our guide on [selling a property in probate](/selling-house-during-probate/) explains the basics, and an attorney can tell you what applies to you.

### 3. The tax status

Find out whether the property taxes are current. In Florida, when property taxes go unpaid, the county tax collector can sell a tax certificate on the property, and if the taxes stay unpaid, the certificate holder can eventually apply for a tax deed. That process can end with the land being sold at auction. If you're behind, the Nassau County Tax Collector's office can tell you exactly what's owed and the deadlines that apply. Back taxes don't always stop a sale; they're often paid off at closing.

### 4. Any surveys, permits, or past paperwork

Old surveys, septic or well permits, or past paperwork can help a buyer understand the property faster.

## Your Options for Selling Vacant Land

There's no single "right" way to sell land. It depends on what matters most to you: price, speed, or simplicity.

### List it with a real estate agent

An agent who knows land can market your property to the public. This can make sense if your land is in a desirable spot and you're willing to wait. Land often takes longer to sell than houses, and you'll typically pay a commission and some closing costs.

### Sell it yourself

You can put up a sign or post it online. You'll save on commission, but you'll handle the calls, the questions, the paperwork, and the buyers who never show up. You'll also want an attorney or title company to handle the closing correctly.
