## Can You Sell a Rental Property With Tenants in Jacksonville?

Yes. In Florida, you can generally sell a house while a tenant is still living in it. What you usually can't do is make the tenant disappear just because the house is being sold. In most cases, the lease stays in place after the sale, and the new owner steps into your shoes as the landlord. That's true whether the tenant is on a one-year lease or renting month to month, though the notice rules differ between the two.

Many buyers don't want to inherit a tenant, especially one who is behind on rent. A family buying a home to live in usually needs it empty, and a lender's appraisal and inspection depend on the tenant letting people inside. That's where a lot of Northside landlords get stuck.

## What the Eviction Process Looks Like in Florida (In Plain Words)

If a tenant isn't paying, the usual first step in Florida is a written three-day notice to pay rent or move out. Those three days don't count weekends or legal holidays. If the tenant doesn't pay or leave, the landlord can file an eviction case in county court. In Duval County, that means the county court here in Jacksonville. The tenant gets served and has a short window to respond. If the court rules for the landlord, the sheriff is the one who carries out the final removal.

A few things are important to know:

- **No "self-help" evictions.** Florida law doesn't allow a landlord to change the locks, remove the tenant's things, or shut off utilities to force someone out. Doing that can create legal trouble for you, even if the tenant owes you money.
- **The paperwork has to be right.** Small mistakes on the notice or the filing can mean starting over.
- **It costs time and money.** There are court filing costs, service fees, and often attorney fees. How long it takes depends on the case and whether the tenant fights it.
- **You may still have repairs afterward.** Some houses come back in rough shape after a tough tenancy.

Again, your attorney is the right person to walk you through the exact steps and timeline for your situation.

## Your Options for Selling a Northside Rental With a Problem Tenant

There isn't one right answer. Here are the paths most landlords look at.

### 1. Finish the Eviction, Then Sell

Some landlords go through the full eviction, wait for the house to be empty, fix it up, and then list it. This can work if you have the time, the money for repairs, and the patience. The downside is that you keep paying the mortgage, taxes, and insurance the whole time, often with no rent coming in.

### 2. Sell With the Tenant in Place to an Investor

Many investors are used to buying houses with tenants. Some will take over a paying tenant and keep the lease going. Others will buy a house where the tenant is behind and handle what comes next themselves, working with their own attorney. This can let you walk away without waiting on the court process to finish. Be clear with any buyer about the tenant situation, the lease, and the security deposit.

### 3. Work Out a Move-Out Agreement

Sometimes a landlord and tenant agree on a move-out date in exchange for something, like forgiving back rent or help with moving costs. This is often called "cash for keys." It can be faster than going to court, but it should be put in writing, and it's smart to have an attorney look at it first.

### 4. Wait for the Lease to End

If your tenant pays and the lease ends soon, you might just wait and sell after it's over. If the tenant is month to month, Florida has notice rules for ending that kind of tenancy, so ask your attorney about the right notice for your case.

## What About the Security Deposit?

If you're holding a security deposit, Florida has rules about how it has to be handled, and those rules don't go away when you sell. Generally, the deposit either goes to the new owner along with the lease, or gets handled with the tenant according to the law. Bring this up early with your attorney and with any buyer so it's settled at closing and nobody is surprised.
