## Can You Sell a House in Bankruptcy in Jacksonville?

In many cases, yes. But once a bankruptcy case is filed, your house usually becomes part of what's called the bankruptcy estate. That means you generally can't just sell it on your own the way you normally would. Selling during bankruptcy usually needs the approval of the bankruptcy trustee, the court, or both.

That approval step is the biggest difference between a normal sale and a bankruptcy sale. It adds paperwork and time, and it's the reason you want your attorney involved from the very first conversation with any buyer.

## How Chapter 7 and Chapter 13 Can Affect the Sale

The two most common types of personal bankruptcy treat your house differently. Here's the general idea.

### Chapter 7

Chapter 7 is often called "liquidation" bankruptcy. A trustee is appointed to look at what you own and decide whether anything can be sold to pay creditors. If your house has equity that isn't protected by an exemption, the trustee may decide to sell it. If there's little or no unprotected equity, the trustee may not be interested in the house at all.

If you want to sell the house yourself during a Chapter 7 case, you'll usually need to work through the trustee. Your attorney can tell you whether that makes sense for your situation.

### Chapter 13

Chapter 13 is a repayment plan, usually spread over several years. Many people file Chapter 13 because they want to keep their house and catch up on missed payments over time. But plans change. A job is lost, the payments get too heavy, or the house needs repairs nobody can afford.

If you're in Chapter 13 and decide to sell, your attorney will usually need to ask the court for permission. The court will want to know the price, who is getting paid from the sale, and how it fits with your repayment plan.

## What About Florida's Homestead Exemption?

Florida has a homestead exemption that can protect a primary residence in bankruptcy, within certain limits. Whether it protects your home, and how much of it, depends on things like how long you've owned and lived in the house and the size of the lot. These rules can get technical, so this is a question for your attorney, not a blog post. Just know that the exemption exists and it can change what happens to your house and your equity.

## Signs a Sale Might Make Sense

Selling isn't the right answer for everyone in bankruptcy. Some people keep their homes and come out the other side just fine. But a sale may be worth discussing with your attorney if:

- You're in Chapter 13 and can't keep up with the plan payments anymore.
- The house needs major repairs you can't afford, like a roof, plumbing or foundation work.
- You're behind on the mortgage and worried about a foreclosure once the case ends.
- You've already moved out and the house is sitting vacant.
- You inherited a house that's adding costs you can't carry.
- You'd rather walk away with a clean slate than keep paying on a house that's dragging you down.

## Why a Traditional Listing Can Be Hard During Bankruptcy

Listing with an agent can still work during bankruptcy, and if you have time and the house is in good shape, it may get you a higher price. But there are a few challenges to think about.

**Repairs and prep cost money.** Most listed homes need cleaning, small fixes, and sometimes big ones before they sell. That's hard to pay for when your finances are being watched closely.

**Buyer financing can fall through.** Many retail buyers need a loan, an appraisal and an inspection. Any one of those can delay or kill the deal, and in a bankruptcy case, a sale that falls apart may mean going back to the court again.

**Time adds up.** Between showings, offers, approvals and closing, the process can stretch out. Meanwhile, interest, taxes, insurance and utilities keep coming.

## Questions to Ask Your Bankruptcy Attorney Before You Sell

Bringing a list to your attorney can save time and money. A few good ones:

- Is my house part of the bankruptcy estate, and does the trustee have any interest in it?
- Does the homestead exemption protect my home or my equity?
- Do I need the trustee's approval, the court's approval, or both to sell?
- How would the money from a sale be divided between my mortgage, my plan and me?
- Are there tax effects from selling that I should ask a tax advisor about?
- How long does the approval process usually take in my case?
